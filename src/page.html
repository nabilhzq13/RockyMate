    3About you

    cm
    cm

    Arm span is fingertip to fingertip with your arms out to the sides. Most people's is about their height; a longer span lets the climber reach further.

    m
    Real size

    Wall height is from the mats to the top edge; most bouldering walls are 4 to 4.5 m, and gyms often post it. Rocksane finds the floor and the top of the wall in the picture and uses this height to draw the climber at your real size. Wall angle is how far the wall leans: a slab leans back, an overhang leans out over you. On an angled wall the holds are further apart than they look from the floor, and the coaching changes with the angle.

      How the wall is read

      Rocksane converts the picture to a colour space that matches how eyes see colour, then estimates the wall's own colour in every small patch. A blob only counts as a hold when it matches the colour of the hold you tapped, clearly differs from the wall right around it, has a real edge, and sits on the same wall. Everything else is shown in red or pink in "Show what matched". The moves come from a search for the fewest, least stretched sequence that starts on the lowest holds and ends with both hands matched on the top hold. It can't see the wall's angle or feel how good a hold is, so treat it as a first plan.

      How to climb a colour route
      
        
      The basics that matter most in your first sessions. Rocksane plans moves with the same ideas: feet first, straight arms, hips in, and both hands on the top hold to finish.
      
      
      
      
      
        
      
          
      How a colour route works
      
          
        
            
      • A route (in bouldering, a problem) uses holds of one colour. Your hands, and at most gyms your feet, stay on that colour.
        • 
            
      • Start: tags or tape mark the start holds. Put one hand on each tagged hold. Two tags on one hold means both hands start there. Then lift your feet off the mat.
        • 
            
      • Finish: bring both hands to the top hold and hold it under control. A quick slap on the way down doesn't count. Some routes finish by climbing over the top instead.
        • 
            
      • Rules vary. Some gyms let you use any foothold on easy routes. Ask staff, then set the feet rule in step 3 to match.
        • 
            
      • Grades use the V-scale (V0, V1, V2…) or Font (4, 5, 6A…). Colour only sometimes means difficulty, so check the tag.
        • 
          
      
        
      
        
      
          
      Read it before you climb
      
          
        
            
      • Stand back and find three things: the start, the finish, and the hardest-looking section (the crux).
        • 
            
      • Look for rests: big holds where you can hang on straight arms and shake out.
        • 
            
      • Trace the moves with your hands from the ground. Decide which hand goes where, then where your feet must be for each reach.
        • 
            
      • Stuck? Practise the crux on its own, watch someone else try it, or test a second method before you give up.
        • 
          
      
        
      
        
      
          
      Footwork first
      
          
        
            
      • Your legs are stronger than your arms. Move your feet up, then stand up to reach, instead of pulling up with your arms.
        • 
            
      • Edging: stand on the hold with the inside edge of your shoe, under the big toe. The outside edge is for back-steps.
        • 
            
      • Smearing: no foothold? Press the sole flat on the wall, heel low, and push into the wall for friction. Works best on walls that lean back.
        • 
            
      • Toe point for pockets and tiny dimples.
        • 
            
      • Silent feet: watch the foothold until your toe is on it and place it without a sound. Quiet feet are accurate feet.
        • 
          
      
        
      
        
      
          
      Body position
      
          
        
            
      • Hang on straight arms. Bent arms tire fast; bend them only to make a move.
        • 
            
      • Keep your hips close to the wall. Turn your knees out on vertical walls; on steep walls, twist one hip into the wall.
        • 
            
      • The best handholds sit roughly above your feet.
        • 
            
      • Barn door: when your hand and foot are both on one side, your body swings open like a door. Fix it with a flag: hold the free leg out the other way as a counterweight.
        • 
            
      • Move like a climber: before a hand moves, shift your hips over your feet and drive up with your legs, then reach. On a foot move, place the foot first, then move your weight onto it.
        • 
            
      • Moves to learn next: drop knee, back-step, stemming, mantle, heel hook, toe hook.
        • 
          
      
        
      
        
      
          
      Hold types, and how to use them
      
          
      Each type asks for a different grip and body position. Tap Show me to watch the climber use it. On your wall, choose Hold type and tap a hold to tell Rocksane what it is.
      
          
      
        
      
        
      
          
      Fall safely
      
          
        
            
      • Warm up shoulders, wrists, hips and fingers before you climb.
        • 
            
      • Check the mat under the route: no bottles, chalk bags or people.
        • 
            
      • Climb down from the top when you can, rather than jumping.
        • 
            
      • If you fall, land on both feet with your knees bent, then sit back and roll. Don't throw your arms out to catch yourself.
        • 
            
      • Never walk or sit under someone who is climbing.
        • 
          
      
        
      
        
      
          
      Words you'll hear
      
          
      
            
      Beta
      The way to climb a route: which holds, which limb, in what order.
      
            
      Crux
      The hardest move or section.
      
            
      Send
      Finish a route without falling.
      
            
      Flash
      Send on your first try, with beta.
      
            
      Onsight
      Send on your first try with no beta at all.
      
            
      Project
      A route you keep working on.
      
            
      Match
      Both hands on one hold.
      
            
      Flag
      A leg held out for balance, not on a hold.
      
            
      Dyno
      A jump to a hold you can't reach statically.
      
            
      Top out
      Finish by climbing over the top of the wall.
