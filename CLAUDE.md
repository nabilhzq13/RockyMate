# Rocksane

A bouldering app. The user points a phone at a gym wall (photo, recorded clip, or live camera), taps one hold of their route, and the app finds every hold of that colour, checks each one really is a hold and not the wall, finds the floor and the top of the wall to get the real scale, then plans the hand and foot sequence to the top. It shows an animated climber at the user's real size (a white mannequin seen from behind), a numbered move list with coaching tips, a Hold type tool (jug, crimp, sloper, pinch, pocket, sidepull, gaston, undercling, volume) that changes the plan and tips, animated technique examples for each hold type, and a "Learn to climb" guide for beginners. It ships as a free web demo, a Claude artifact, and an iPhone and Android app (Capacitor) with a one-time **Rocksane Pro** purchase.

## How detection works (src/vision.js)

1. `prepare(rgba,w,h)`: sRGB to CIELAB. Local wall model: grid cells of about 1/18 of the long side; each cell's wall colour is the mode of a coarse Lab histogram refined by 3 mean passes; cells that differ from their 3x3 neighbourhood median by more than 11 are replaced (a big hold filling a cell). Bilinear per-pixel wall map. `dfg` = distance from the local wall (L weighted 0.5). `Tfg` = clamp(2.8 x median(dfg) + 5, 9, 24) adapts to wall texture. `gRef` = median colour gradient.
2. `pick(I,x,y)`: from the tapped point, grow the hold region (pixels that differ from the wall and are close to the seed colour). Build the colour model from **core** pixels only (not the rim, which mixes hold and wall colour, and only pixels with dfg >= 1.3 Tfg). Model = Lab mean + covariance, chroma-weighted hue mean and spread, chroma, L range, plus the wall colour in a ring around the tapped hold.
3. `classify(I,model,tol,{mask,pxPerCm})`, tol 1-10 (UI slider, default 5):
   - Gates: hue within clamp(3 x hueSd, 10, 28) x (0.5 + 0.1 tol) degrees, chroma >= max(8, Cm x (0.55 - 0.025 tol)); achromatic models (chroma < 11: black, white, grey) gate on low chroma and L range instead. Then Mahalanobis <= (2 + 0.35 tol)^2.
   - Wall test per pixel: must differ from the local wall by more than Tfg AND be closer to the route model (in its sigma) than to the wall (sigma = Tfg/3). Pixel classes: 1 route, 2 right colour but wall.
   - Morphology: close then open, radius about long side / 420.
   - Blob scoring: contrast vs the ring of wall around the blob, edge strength at the boundary vs `gRef`, shape (fill vs ellipse, elongation), ring similar to the tapped hold's wall, inside the wall region (grid cells similar to that wall colour, largest connected parts, dilated), not the edge of a painted area of the same colour (ring share of class 2 > 0.25), size 2-110 cm when a scale is known. conf >= 0.5 hold, >= 0.33 unsure (dashed amber, user can keep it), else rejected with a reason string. Each blob also carries `ring` (the wall colour around it).
   - With `mask:true` it returns per-pixel classes for the "Show what matched" view: 1 kept, 2 wall look-alike (red), 3 rejected blob (pink), 4 unsure (amber).
4. `refine()` refits the model from confident holds (photos only). `suggest()` makes the colour chips: k-means on pixels that stand out from the wall, keeps colours that form 2 or more hold blobs, ranks by count.

## Real size (wallFrame in vision.js, autoCal in app.js)

Before this, the whole picture counted as 4.2 m of wall, so a photo taken from further back drew the climber far too big (1.59x on average on the benchmark) and let it reach the top in impossible moves.

- `wallFrame(I,{W,holds})` finds the **floor line** (where the wall meets the mats) and the **top line** (the wall's top edge). The picture is cut into 20 vertical strips; in each strip a per-strip wall colour and row-median colour edges give candidate rows; RANSAC fits a line through candidates across strips, with checks: the wall colour must hold for 12% of the height on the wall side and not on the far side, the line must overlap the route's width, holds whose ring isn't wall-like are ignored, a floor must have wall above it and a top wall below it, and of several lines the one nearest the route wins. Returns `{floor, top, strips}`, each line `{m, c, n, of, sc}` in pixels or null. About 46 ms at 640 px.
- `app.js` keeps lines as `v = a + m·u` (normalized). `autoCal()`: the user's lines if they dragged them; the sample wall is the whole picture; otherwise the found lines. A top line is dropped when more than max(1, 10%) of the route's holds sit above it (counting a hold's centre plus half its radius). A missing floor is put 30 cm below the lowest hold; a missing top is the picture's top edge; a wall thinner than 0.3 of the picture falls back to the picture's bottom. The scale `kU()` = (floor - top) / (wall height in cm), measured at the route's mean x (`routeU()`). Each plan keeps the calibration it was made with (`S.beta.cal`), so the drawing matches the plan while lines are dragged.
- Live and clips: edges are re-read every second, blended at 0.4, and dropped after 3 misses; the plan is redone when the floor moves more than 0.02 of the height or the span changes more than 4%.
- **Check size** tool (`size` mode, the last tool; works before a colour is picked): the floor and top lines with handles, labels away from the climber, and a standing climber at the route with an "H cm" bracket, so the user can judge the scale. Dragging keeps each line's slope and at least 0.15 between them; Reset returns to the found edges. `SIZENOTE` explains per case (edges, floor only, top only, none, the user's marks, sample) in Settings.
- Body proportions behind the reach (anthropometric data: maximum overhead reach about 1.36 x height): arm reach 0.5H from a shoulder 0.3H above the hips, legs 0.56H.
- Weak spots: a window or wall area of the route's colour above the wall can drop a correct top (img1 blue: 1.23x); overhanging boulders and steep facets are ambiguous; perspective (camera tilt) is not corrected.

## How the moves are planned (src/solver.js)

- Units: cm, Y along the wall from the floor (the app divides picture heights by cos(wall angle), since an angled wall is longer than it looks from the floor). Body from height H and arm span (`o.span`, default H): arm reach ARM = 0.5H + (span - H)/2 from a shoulder point TORSO = 0.3H above the hip, leg reach 0.56H. A pose is feasible if the hip fits inside the intersection of the reach disks (candidates: ideal hip, projections onto each disk, pairwise circle intersections). Ideal hip: x between the hands and the feet (feet weighted 1.5), y = average foot + 0.46H, capped so the shoulders are at most 0.04H above the lowest hand.
- Pose cost follows coaching rules (sources: John Baker "Face climbing principles", Philadelphia Rock Gyms "Mastering foot sequencing", climber.co.uk technique tips): distance from the ideal hip, limb stretch beyond 0.78 (arms beyond 0.92 extra), feet close to the hands (gap < 0.7H), feet high under the hips (< 0.3H below the hip), stance wider than 0.42H, hips not over the feet, an arm reaching across the body, and 1.2 per flagged foot (flags are short-term).
- A* over states (left hand, right hand, left foot, right foot); a foot can be -1 (flag) or on floor points at the start. Both feet must leave the floor before a hand moves. Foot move 0.5 + 0.8 (d/LEG)^2 (+ smear, matching feet 2.0 on small holds, stepping down); flag 1.8; hand move 1 + 0.6 (d/ARM)^2 + pose (+ matching 1.5 on small holds, moving down, barn-door penalty when the other hand and the feet don't hold the hips from both sides).
- Hands never cross and feet never cross, in every pass (the owner does not want tangled limbs; on the benchmark this costs no routes).
- Wall angle (`o.angle`, degrees, + overhang, - slab): overhangs lower the preferred foot-to-hand gap (0.7H - 0.2H x steepness, steepness = angle/45) and the feet-under-hips distance, and make hand moves up to 1.5x as costly; slabs (angle/-20) allow higher feet and make hand moves cheaper. `buildBeta()` adds a slab or overhang tip to the start and toe-pull tips on steep walls.
- Strict pass first: feet at least 0.5H below the lowest hand and not above the hip, hands at least 0.45H above the feet. Tried from the 3 most natural starts, sharing one search budget. If none reaches the top, a relaxed pass (feet 0.15H below the hands, high steps allowed but costed by the pose rules, hands 0.12H above the feet) searches from every start pair at once. `res.strict` says which pass won. On real photos footholds are sparse, so the relaxed pass usually decides; the sample wall solves strict.
- Start (no tags): pairs from the 8 lowest holds between 0.35H and 1.3H, scored by closeness to chest height (0.8H), level hands, footholds at least 0.5H below, any foothold at all, and rank; a pair is "natural" (strict may use it) when the hands are within 0.6H of each other and between 0.45H and 1.2H. Or the user's Set start holds. Top = highest hold you can chain to from the low holds within 1.1H, or the user's Set top. Heuristic = remaining height / 0.42H, weight 1.4 (1.8 open feet), max 26k expansions per pass (22k open feet). Returns the best partial path when stuck. If neither pass reaches the top, a last relaxed pass lets a hand go up to 6% past ARM (a deadpoint, steep cost above 1.0 stretch; tip "push hard with your legs and catch the hold as you rise"). `res.stretch` marks it.
- The app (`solveNow`): calls `autoCal()` first; if the route can't be finished on footholds of the route colour, it solves again with open feet (smears) and keeps that plan if it gets further, with a note in the move panel (`res.autoSmear`). Small footholds are often missed in pictures; this is what turns most "stuck at the start" routes into full plans. With the real scale some routes are honestly out of reach for a 170 cm climber; the move panel says so and suggests checking the size and the wall angle.
- Hold types (`h.type`, only the ones the user set; sources: Atomik "basic types of climbing handholds", Climbing "How to climb slopers" and "How to undercling", Earth Treks "What is a gaston"): a hand move onto a typed hold costs extra by quality (jug 0, sidepull 0.25, pinch 0.3, crimp and undercling 0.35, pocket and volume 0.4, sloper and gaston 0.45) and so does a foot on one (sloper 0.35, pocket and undercling 0.25, pinch 0.2, crimp, sidepull and gaston 0.15). Pose rules per hand: sloper wants a straight arm (stretch at least 0.85), undercling between 0.38H below the shoulders and shoulder height, sidepull the hips at least 0.12H to the side of it (lean away), gaston within 0.2H of the hips sideways and at most 0.15H above the shoulders; a pocket or crimp hand with a flagged foot costs 0.6. On the benchmark, typing every hold with its guess changes no top count (`TYPES=guess`).
- `buildBeta()` numbers holds by first use, writes captions ("Right hand → hold 7 · 45 cm up · sloper", or "· looks like a jug" for a guess), tips (the set type's grip and body position first, then push through the same-side foot and hang off the other hand, barn door and flag, long reach, match, rest, high step, smear, first step off the mat, footwork), `how` (hold types this step can show an example for) and `reach`, and marks the crux (costliest hand move). A rest needs two big holds or two the user marked as jugs or volumes. `guessType()` guesses from size and shape: 30 cm or more volume, long and upright sidepull, long and flat or under 12 cm crimp, upright and a bit long pinch, else jug.

## The climber figure (drawClimber in src/app.js)

- Drawn from behind, facing the wall, as a white mannequin: capsules for limbs, a shaped torso with spine, shoulder blades, waistline and glutes, knee creases, the nape of the head, elbow points, and dark climbing shoes with the toe on the hold and the heel toward the viewer. Its size comes from the real scale (above).
- Layer order is arms, then body, then head: seen from behind, the arms reach past the body toward the wall, so the back and head cover them where they overlap (a hand in front of the chest is hidden; its L/R badge still marks the hold).
- `chooseBody()` picks how the planner's pose is drawn: the hips may sink up to 0.15H (0.2H while a foot is on the mat, never closer than 0.22H above the higher foot) and the upper body leans up to 0.5 rad. Cost: lean away from half the angle to the hands, lean beyond 0.3 rad, sinking, a hand beyond arm's length, a hand closer to the head than about 1.25 head radii, an arm segment overlapping the head, and a hand on the wrong side of the head around head height (not for matched hands). Coarse-to-fine grid search, about 1-2 ms, memoized per pose. While a move animates, `bodyFor()` blends lean and sink between the two held positions so the torso never flips.
- The drawn arm is 0.96 x ARM; `reachJoint()` lifts the shoulder up to 0.05H toward a hand at full stretch. On an angled wall, `cmPx()` multiplies heights by cos(angle), so the climber is foreshortened like the wall.
- Elbows pick the outward-and-down solution; knees turn out. `bend3d()` keeps only part of the flat sideways swing (arms 0.55, legs 0.35) because real joints bend toward the wall.
- Each layer is filled, edge-shaded with an inner shadow from an inverted mask, then composited with drop shadows (drawn at half resolution: `LOWRES`, `lowCopy`, `shadowOnly`) into an offscreen sprite that is only redrawn when the pose or view changes. The sprite is drawn without camera drift and blitted at the drift offset; while a move plays it is drawn at up to 1.5x instead of the full device pixel ratio. `figureGeom()`, `paintFigure()` and `drawLimbBadges()` are shared by the wall view and the technique examples.
- Move timing (`blendPose()`; sources: Flash and Hogan 1985 minimum-jerk model of reaching, Zampagni et al. 2011 on centre-of-mass shifts in expert climbers, coaching advice to move the hips first): each limb and the hips follow a minimum-jerk profile. On a hand move the hips lead (0 to 70% of the move) and the hand follows (22 to 100%) on a curved path that swings out away from the body; on a foot move the foot lifts and goes first (0 to 62%) and the hips follow (30 to 100%). Duration 0.65 to 1.15 s by distance (560 ms + 5 ms per cm). A deadpoint (`reach > 1`) is faster with the hips rising 0.035H. `sag` swings the hips through low (the sloper example). A move started mid-move starts from what is on screen.

## Drawing speed

- `render()` keeps two cached layers: `R.ov` (the picture and the hold rings) and `R.lab` (number tags and labels), rebuilt only when `overlaySig()` changes (holds, edits, step, mode, size, mask via `maskStamp`); `fit()` clears `R.sig`. The climber sprite (above) is the only per-frame work while the moves play. Optional per-render timings in `S.perf`.
- Measured in headless Chromium at phone size: playing the moves at 60 fps (was 43), 38 fps with 4x CPU slowdown (was 25).

## Phone layout and iOS-style motion (src/app.js, src/page.html)

- **App mode** (`setAppMode`): on in the app, and in a browser narrower than 760 px (`appMQ`). An app bar (`#appbar`: camera icon → New picture sheet, the logo, Learn, Settings) over the wall; a **bottom sheet** (`#bsheet`) with three detents (peek, half, full) holds the player, the tools (`#pTools`), the move caption, then the Route colour, Your beta and coach blocks. `place()` moves existing blocks into sheets: the wall sources into New picture (`#msNewBody`), About you and Rocksane Pro into Settings (`#msSetBody`), the Learn view into the Learn sheet. `appLayout()` sizes the detents from the safe areas (peek 222 px + bottom inset, half 56% of the height, full to under the bar) and the wall above the peeked sheet; `fit()` uses that height. The hold type picker becomes a panel at the bottom.
- **Springs** (Apple, WWDC23 "Animate with springs"): `Spring(x)` is a closed-form damped spring with stiffness (2π/duration)² and damping ratio 1 - bounce; retargeting keeps the current velocity, so a flick carries into the settle. `SPRING_CSS` is a CSS `linear()` easing sampled from the same spring. Drags follow the finger with a rubber band past the ends (`rubber(over,dim)` = over·dim·0.55/(dim+0.55|over|), UIScrollView's formula), the release velocity comes from the last 100 ms (`Tracker`), and the resting detent is chosen from the projected end point (`project(v)`, deceleration 0.996 as in UIScrollView). A tap on the grabber cycles the detents.
- **Sheets** (`Modal(el,{onOpen,onClose})`): New picture, Settings, Learn, Rocksane Pro and the technique example slide up on a spring; drag the head down to close (closes past 42% of the height or above 1100 px/s, projected); stacked sheets get rising z-index; in app mode the page behind scales to 94% with rounded corners like an iOS card; Escape closes the top sheet, Tab stays inside, focus returns to the opener. `popIn()` for toasts and the picker.
- **Haptics**: `haptic.sel/tap/thud/ok/warn` through the bridge (selection on steps and detents, impacts on edits, success or warning when a plan comes); in Android browsers `navigator.vibrate` stands in (`vib()`; iOS browsers have no vibration API).
- **Safe areas**: CSS uses `var(--safe-area-inset-X, env(safe-area-inset-X, 0px))`, since Capacitor's SystemBars plugin (`insetsHandling: css`) injects `--safe-area-inset-*`.

## App behaviour (src/app.js)

- Coordinates: holds are normalized (`u` = x / width, `v` = y / height, `rho` = radius / height). `S.cal` holds the floor and top lines (`fl`, `tp` as `{a, m}`), their values at the route (`floor`, `top`), the source (`edges`, `floor`, `top`, `picture`, `marks`, `sample`) and whether the user marked them; with the wall height in metres it gives `kU()` = height units per cm.
- Workers: a vision worker (`VisionCore` + `visionHandler`, messages `frame` / `classify` / `pick`, tagged with the source token so stale results are dropped; `frame` can also return the wall's edges) and a solver worker. Both fall back to running in the page if a Worker can't start.
- Tapping the wall: modes `pick` (learn the colour from the tapped hold), `none` (colour picked, no tool: a tap only nudges the tool buttons, so a stray tap can't re-learn the colour and change the path), the tools `add` (add a missed hold on a still picture, keep a dashed amber one, or put back a crossed-out removed one), `remove`, `type`, `start` (one or two holds; tap again to unset), `top` (tap again to unset), and `size` (drag the floor and top lines). The hold tools are disabled until a colour is picked; tapping the active tool turns it off.
- A new clip or the live camera keeps the colour only if it was picked on the user's own picture (`ownColour()`), never the sample wall's green; closing a clip back to the sample re-picks the sample's green. While a clip or the camera runs, colour chips are suggested every 2.5 s until a colour is picked and every 6 s after; once a colour is picked, suggestions of that same colour are hidden.
- Picking the colour again keeps the start, top and hold edits (and hold types) when it is the same route (`sameRoute()`: same colour name, or hue within the model's hue window and chroma ratio 0.55 to 1.8, or brightness for black/white/grey); a different colour clears them with a toast. Choosing a start or top also confirms that hold.
- Photo: analysed at 640 px long side. User edits (removed, kept, added, start, top, types) are stored as normalized positions so they survive re-detection; start and top match only the hold within 1.5 radii of the saved spot.
- Clip and live: requestAnimationFrame draws every frame; frames at 400 px go to the worker at most every 70 ms once a colour is chosen. Tracking matches blobs to tracks by nearest neighbour (gate max(0.03, 2.2 rho)) with a median-shift prediction and running averages; a track is shown after 3 hits. Status has hysteresis (a hold stays a hold down to confidence 0.42, unsure down to 0.28); a track with 10 or more hits stays shown through 4 missed scans, or 12 near the edge of the view. In live mode edits live on track ids: marked tracks survive 30 missed scans instead of 7; a start or top stays in the plan through 12 missed scans; a dropped track is remembered for 15 s in `S.lost` and a blob that reappears there gets its old id and edits back; a dropped marked track hands its id to a new track already following the same hold. The beta is re-solved when the set of holds has been stable for 0.8 s and the player is not playing; the first plan also comes 2 s after the colour is picked, and a later re-plan when at least two holds differ from the planned set for 2 s or the wall's edges move (above); no re-plan within 10 s of the user stepping through the moves (`S.navT`). The climber drawing follows camera drift. Freeze frame turns the current frame into a photo and carries edits, types and size lines over.
- Hold type tool: tap a ringed hold and a picker opens next to it (a bottom panel in app mode). It shows the guess (dashed button), the nine types with icons, a one-line summary, "Show how" (opens that type's example) and "Use the guess". Types are stored like the other edits and reach the solver; set types show as a white tag under the hold; with the tool on, untyped holds show their guess with a question mark.
- Technique examples (`openDemo(type, opener)`): a sheet with its own canvas (216 x 288 cm of wall), the same climber (170 cm, `DEMOS` keys with `fixHip`), a caption per step, prev / play-pause / next, the grip and body tips, and chips to switch type. `shake` makes a free hand shake out (jug), `force` draws a yellow arrow for the pull or push. Reduced motion: no autoplay. Opened from the move caption ("How to use a sloper"), the picker, and the Learn guide.
- "Ask Claude" uses `window.claude.use('sample')` with an image; it only exists inside Claude and is hidden elsewhere. Call-outs use `speechSynthesis`. Height, arm span, wall height, wall angle and feet rule persist in `localStorage`.

## Constraints to keep

- One self-contained HTML file per edition. The artifact viewer blocks camera, microphone, `alert/confirm/prompt`, downloads, `window.print`, and network requests to other hosts; scripts may only load from cdnjs, jsdelivr, unpkg, cdn.tailwindcss.com or code.jquery.com; stylesheets only from Google Fonts. Toggle visibility with the `hidden` attribute.
- The web, dev and app editions load nothing from other hosts (fonts are self-hosted): the privacy policy says so.
- Keep `VisionCore` and `solveBeta` free of outside references (they run inside workers via `toString()`).
- Colours come from CSS tokens on `:root` with the two dark-mode blocks; don't add literal colours to components.
- UI copy: plain words, active voice, short sentences; errors say what happened and what to do.
- App Review and Play rules: Pro is unlocked only by the store purchase, with Restore; prices come from the store; the app names only its own platform's store; no data leaves the phone (the App Store and Play data forms say "no data collected"); keep `ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption` false and the target API level current.
- After any detection change, run `node test/eval.js`; after any change to wall edges or scale, run `node test/scale.js` and `SCALE=auto node test/plan.js`; after any planner or figure change, run `node test/plan.js` (keep the top count at least the v1 planner's); after any UI change, run `python test/e2e.py` (and `python test/app_edition.py` for anything touching Pro or the bridge).

## Known gaps and next ideas

- Holds the same colour as the wall cannot be separated by colour alone; dual-colour holds, heavy chalk and busy spray walls are weak spots.
- Real size assumes the camera faces the wall square-on; a tilted photo stretches the top or the bottom (next: a 4-point perspective correction from the floor and top lines plus the wall's sides). Overhanging boulders with several facets confuse the top line.
- Start tags (tape) are not detected; auto start = lowest reachable holds.
- Tracking has no zoom or rotation compensation; fast pans reset tracks.
- The wall angle is one number for the whole picture; the solver ignores dynos beyond a 6% deadpoint, heel and toe hooks, drop knees and back-steps.
- Hold type guesses come from size and shape only; which way a sidepull or gaston faces is not known.
- Colour names come from the hue: golden yellow holds can be named orange.
- Ideas (from a look at Visionclimb, Beta Sprayer, BoulderBot, KAYA, Stōkt, Kilter, TopLogger and Crimpd): a route log per gym with sends and attempts; video form check of the user's own climb with pose tracking against the plan; coaching scaled to the user's level; a grade estimate; feet-only holds and start/finish tags; a problem generator for home and spray walls; sharing a route's beta as a link; a high-contrast, spoken mode for low-vision climbers.
